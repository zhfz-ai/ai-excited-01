# Judge Scorecard — "The Night He Hooked the Moon"

Self-score against the five judge criteria, in order. Each criterion is
scored 0–2.

## 1. Jolt of energy / awe — 2

A man fishing the moon out of the sky lands the whole idea in one glance:
the bent rod, the taut line, and the huge glowing moon do all the work at
once.

## 2. Surprise — 2

The moon is where the fish should be, and the joke only lands a beat after
you see the rod — the picture rewards the second look instead of spending
itself immediately.

## 3. Momentum — 1

The bent rod and the rising line pull the eye upward hard, but the boat
itself is still, so the motion lives in the line rather than in the scene —
the picture is a held breath, not a chase.

## 4. Craft — 2

One subject, one light source, one clean diagonal from boat to moon; the
dark sea stays empty on purpose so the composition never gets crowded.

## 5. Reading small — 2

At thumbnail size the silhouette still reads: tiny boat at the bottom, bent
rod, giant bright disc at the top — the whole gag survives at 300 pixels.

**Total: 9 / 10**

## Alternatives considered

- **A cat batting the moon like a ball of yarn.** Charming and funny, but
  the joke is familiar — cats and yarn are a well-worn pairing, so the awe
  fades fast.
- **A librarian shelving a miniature supernova.** Clever and strange, but it
  needs a caption to explain itself, and the wonder gets lost in the setup.

The fisherman wins on criterion 1 because his awe is the picture's engine:
one ordinary man meeting something impossibly large, with his face lit up
by it — the viewer feels the jolt instead of having to decode it.
